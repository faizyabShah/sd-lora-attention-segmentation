# Attention Segmentation Analysis

## Executive summary

This report explains the VOC-Sim results over **490 annotated images and 20 classes**. It consolidates **78,108 evaluated configurations** (counting the exhaustive layer subsets) and adds image-level diagnostics without running another model-selection sweep.

Our end-to-end implementation of unoptimized OVAM reached **0.6934 mIoU**. The authors' released masks reach **0.7036**, which is retained as a reproduction check rather than substituted for our baseline. The best focused seed/operator result reached **0.7922**, joint head/time tuning reached **0.7991** without morphology, and removing predicted components below 1% of the image produced the final **0.7997 mIoU**. The final result is an absolute gain of **0.1064** over our implemented OVAM (15.3% relative).

The final method improves pooled IoU for **18/20 classes** and image IoU for **376/490 images** (0 ties). Its paired mean image-IoU gain is **+0.0999**, with a bootstrap 95% interval of **[+0.0845, +0.1151]** (20,000 image resamples; seed 20261012).

### Main findings

- **Self-attention propagation is the main contributor.** In the matched top-four-seed path it adds +0.1092 mIoU; raw CA head selection alone adds only +0.0001.
- **The gain is mainly better false-positive control.** False-positive pixels fall by 63.2%, while recall also rises from 0.895 to 0.897.
- **CA and SA favor different U-Net regions.** Coarse/middle CA gives the cleanest semantic seed; late 64×64 decoder SA gives the best spatial propagation.
- **Iteration, time, and heads matter after choosing the operator.** Four iterations add +0.0192, late steps add +0.0054, and omitting heads 3/7 adds +0.0062 mIoU in matched comparisons.
- **Cleanup is minor.** Small-component removal adds only +0.0006 mIoU.
- **Class effects are uneven.** The five largest pooled-IoU gains occur for chair, sofa, train, bicycle, dining-table; classes that regress are car, bus.

## Experimental protocol

Each image was regenerated with its released prompt, seed, 30 inference steps, Stable Diffusion 1.5, and guidance scale 7.5. Attention was analyzed in memory. After scalar measurements and an optional visual panel were written, all Q/K tensors, affinity matrices, latents, and temporary maps were released. No raw attention tensor dataset was saved.

Qualitative panels use the released OVAM image beside attention captured from our reproduction. Earlier reproduction checks found very small pixel-level differences caused by the software/runtime stack, while composition and mask geometry remained aligned; the released annotation is the evaluation target throughout.

The OVAM baseline in every comparison is computed by our code from the regenerated trajectory. It aggregates ordinary-prompt cross-attention; reproduces the self-attention hook across guidance branches, heads, timesteps, and only the highest-resolution 64×64 blocks required by Eq. 8; rescales SA to [0.85, 1]; multiplies CA×SA; thresholds at 0.40; and applies the authors' DenseCRF parameters to the regenerated RGB image. Token optimization is excluded. The authors' released masks (0.7036) and released CA-only result (0.6616) are reported only to quantify reproduction fidelity.

The final method uses the fixed top-four cross-attention heads in each of the previously selected 12 layers, the final 64×64 self-attention module, four propagation iterations, self-attention heads 0/1/2/4/5/6, denoising steps 15–30, percentile 1–99 normalization, threshold 0.45, and removal of connected components smaller than 1% of the image.

## What the diagnostic measures mean

- **IoU and average precision** measure spatial localization. Average precision is threshold-free and helps distinguish map quality from the chosen 0.45 cutoff.
- **Normalized entropy and effective support** measure spread. Lower values mean concentration, but concentration is useful only when it falls on the object.
- **Foreground/background gap and ROC AUC** measure how well attention ranks object pixels above background pixels.
- **Boundary gradient ratio** measures whether changes in the map concentrate around the annotated object boundary.
- **Centroid error** measures displacement between attention mass and the target center.
- **Object area, box occupancy, components, boundary fraction, and compactness** describe class geometry and let us test whether improvements are dominated by large or simple objects.
- For implemented OVAM, ranking statistics describe our live fused CA×SA map before thresholding; mask IoU and predicted area use our own post-DenseCRF output.

### Which changes actually mattered

The following are matched comparisons: each row changes one stated factor while holding the listed pipeline context fixed. They are more informative than comparing unrelated family winners.

| Controlled change | Before | After | mIoU change | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| All 16 CA heads/layers → selected top-4-head seed, before SA | 0.6830 | 0.6831 | +0.0001 | Raw seed selection alone is negligible |
| Top-4 CA seed → two iterations through final 64×64 SA | 0.6831 | 0.7922 | +0.1092 | SA propagation is the dominant improvement |
| One → four SA propagation iterations, same heads/time/threshold | 0.7799 | 0.7991 | +0.0192 | Repeated propagation materially improves object coverage |
| All timesteps → steps 15–30, same module/heads/iterations | 0.7937 | 0.7991 | +0.0054 | Removing noisy early steps gives a moderate gain |
| All SA heads → omit heads 3 and 7 | 0.7929 | 0.7991 | +0.0062 | Head complementarity matters after propagation |
| Min-max → percentile 1–99 normalization | 0.7904 | 0.7922 | +0.0018 | Calibration helps, but is secondary |
| No morphology → remove components below 1% | 0.7991 | 0.7997 | +0.0006 | Morphology contributes only the last small increment |

The central result is therefore not simply better thresholding or cleanup. The large controlled jump occurs when the semantic CA seed is propagated through late high-resolution self-attention. Iteration count, timestep restriction, and the joint head subset refine that operator. Normalization and morphology make comparatively small adjustments. These effects interact and were selected on VOC-Sim, so the row-wise gains should not be added as if they were independent.

### OVAM reproduction fidelity

Against the authors' released intermediate files, our live cross-attention has mean per-image Pearson correlation **0.965**, the self-attention reduction has correlation **0.237**, and the fused CA×SA map has correlation **0.962**. Our post-CRF masks overlap the released post-CRF masks at mean image IoU **0.930**. These values measure software-level reproduction fidelity; segmentation accuracy is reported separately above.

The live SA reduction contains **18.9 distinct float values per image on average**, while the released normalized SA arrays contain only **5.5**. This matters because the authors' hook applies attention to an all-ones value tensor. In exact arithmetic every attention row returns ones, so the spatial signal comes from finite-precision differences that are amplified by min-max normalization. We preserve the released operation for baseline fidelity, but it should not be interpreted as a conventional self-attention saliency map.

### Where the pixel errors changed

Across all pixels, precision rises from **0.710** to **0.869** and recall rises from **0.895** to **0.897**. False-positive pixels fall by **63.2%**, false-negative pixels fall by **1.9%**, and true-positive pixels rise by **0.2%**. The main aggregate mechanism is suppression of excess foreground, with a smaller simultaneous recovery of missed object pixels.

## Why cross-attention selection helps

The strongest individual CA layer is the 8×8 middle block `mid_block.attentions.0.transformer_blocks.0.attn2` (0.569 mean image IoU), followed by the two 16×16 down-block layers (0.559 and 0.552). Several late 64×64 CA layers score only about 0.17–0.21. This supports using coarse and middle CA for semantic localization: high spatial resolution does not make a CA layer semantically cleaner. The top-four-head seed has slightly lower entropy and changes centroid error from **0.046** to **0.045**, but its standardized foreground/background gap changes from **1.804** to **1.796**. Its raw mIoU advantage over all 16 layers is only +0.0001; its value is primarily compatibility with downstream SA propagation.

![Cross-attention layers](outputs/analysis/voc_sim_attention_report_implemented_ovam_highres/figures/ca_layer_iou.svg)

## Why the final 64×64 self-attention module helps

Applying the same seed, four iterations, and late-step window to every SA module identifies `up_blocks.3.attentions.2.transformer_blocks.0.attn1` as best (0.796 mean image IoU). The adjacent late 64×64 upsampling module reaches 0.794, whereas the 8×8 middle SA module reaches only 0.620. Resolution alone is still insufficient: early 64×64 downsampling SA modules reach only 0.717–0.745. The useful combination is high resolution plus a late decoder position, after global layout has been integrated and spatial detail has been restored.

For the final selected map, normalized entropy is **0.937**, effective support is **0.617**, foreground/background standardized gap is **1.976**, boundary gradient ratio is **3.951**, and centroid error is **0.037**. Across images, final-map entropy and IoU have Spearman **ρ=-0.180** (p=6.34e-05); entropy alone is therefore interpreted together with alignment metrics.

## Why heads 3 and 7 are removed

Under the final module and late-step window, the best individual head is head **5** (0.784 mean image IoU). The matched all-head configuration scores 0.7929; omitting heads 3 and 7 raises it to 0.7991. Heads 3 and 7 average **0.751** alone versus **0.767** for retained heads, but retained head 4 is weaker alone than either excluded head. The exclusion is therefore a complementarity result, not a valid universal ranking of individual heads.

## Why later denoising steps help

The mean individual-step IoU is **0.670** for steps 0–14 and **0.799** for steps 15–30. It rises from 0.438 at step 0 to a peak of 0.802 around steps 17–21, then falls to 0.786 at step 30. Boundary alignment peaks slightly earlier, around step 14. This is consistent with a transition from noisy coarse composition to stable object geometry, followed by mild late overspecialization. Averaging steps 15–30 improves the matched all-step configuration by +0.0054 mIoU and reduces dependence on any single step.

## Interpretation limits

- These are exploratory ablations and explanations on VOC-Sim. Configuration selection and final reporting use the same 490 images, so the 0.7997 result needs confirmation on the other datasets already planned.
- Individual layer, head, and step profiles isolate one component at a time. They explain association with performance but do not prove a unique causal mechanism because attention components interact.
- VOC-Sim contains generated, usually centered objects and binary foreground masks. Conclusions about clutter, multiple instances, and natural-image class imbalance require external validation.
- The released images are not byte-identical to our regenerated pixels under the current software stack. Attention is captured from the seeded reproduction, while scoring uses the released spatial mask; the earlier direct checks showed aligned composition and mask geometry.
- Map entropy has no universal optimum. Very diffuse and very concentrated maps can both fail; foreground separation, boundary alignment, centroid error, and IoU provide the needed spatial context.
