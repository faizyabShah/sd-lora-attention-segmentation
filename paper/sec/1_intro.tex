\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Diffusion models generate an image while repeatedly connecting text tokens to image positions and image positions to one another. Cross-attention (CA) offers a semantic seed for a queried class; self-attention (SA) can carry that evidence across the object's extent. DAAM aggregates CA for attribution~\cite{tang2023daam}, while OVAM extends attribution to words absent from the generation prompt and studies SA fusion and CRF refinement~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}. This makes generated-image segmentation possible without fitting a segmentation network.

The difficulty is selecting \emph{which} attention to use. Stable Diffusion 1.5 exposes 31 denoising calls, four spatial scales, sixteen CA and sixteen SA modules, and eight heads per module, and the attention graph can be propagated, filtered or re-weighted in many ways. A combination tuned on the images used to choose it may look better than it is, and a restriction that helps alone may stop helping once another component changes. We therefore treat the design as a sequence of questions, each answered on five VOC-Sim folds and only then transferred, frozen, to COCO-Cap.

The sequence has three roles (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline_stages}). CA must find a semantic core of the queried class; SA must grow that core into the object without leaking into the background; and an image-aware refinement must sharpen the boundary. The answers led to a simple training-free pipeline: average CA over the non-32-pixel modules, propagate the seed through the last 64-pixel SA module twice within each call--head observation, and apply OVAM's fixed DenseCRF. It scores 0.813 out-of-fold (OOF) mean IoU on VOC-Sim and 0.637 on COCO-Cap.

The route there is part of the result (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision_map}). Of 45 registered comparisons, one per experiment family and stage, most did not survive the fold rule, and several plausible graph operators were harmful. A family of observation-reliability operators did improve one-pass propagation, by about one point, yet none improved on a second pass and none survived DenseCRF: they were substitutes for object completion, not additions to it. This pattern changed how we selected. Later studies chose settings \emph{after} refinement, which uncovered the one change that survives the full pipeline: restricting SA to calls 10--20, selected in four of five folds (0.816 OOF, 0.644 COCO-Cap). Later CA-weighting and multi-scale studies raise COCO-Cap transfer but are not selected on VOC-Sim; we report them as measured extensions with explicit selection status.

The paper contributes a traceable selection protocol for CA, SA and post-processing; a staged analysis of semantic seeding, object completion and leakage, with the negative results that shaped it; and a published-baseline comparison that separates regenerated-image results from the original paper's results.
