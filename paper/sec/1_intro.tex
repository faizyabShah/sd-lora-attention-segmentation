\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A diffusion model that draws ``a photograph of a sheep'' must decide, at every denoising step, which image positions belong to the word \emph{sheep} and which positions belong together. The first decision is visible in cross-attention (CA) between image queries and text keys; the second in self-attention (SA) between image positions. Reading both out yields a segmentation mask of the generated image without any training~\cite{tang2023daam,marcosmanchon2024ovam}, which makes generated images a source of free pixel labels.

Turning attention into masks is nevertheless a design problem with many degrees of freedom. Stable Diffusion~1.5 exposes 31 denoising calls, CA and SA at four spatial scales, sixteen modules of each type and eight heads per module. Existing pipelines average CA over modules, propagate it once with fine SA and refine the result~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}. Recent work adds re-weighting, graph sharpening or random walks on top of that recipe~\cite{meng2026making,mahatha2025nerve,mahatha2026spark,park2025seediff}. We tested many such operators under a strict protocol, and most did not help. What did help was respecting the different roles that attention plays at different \emph{scales} (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}):
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \textbf{Seed.} CA at 8 and 16 pixels marks the queried object reliably but coarsely; 64-pixel CA adds detail but is noisy, and 32-pixel CA hurts. Weighting modules by how well their spatial structure agrees with SA puts 84\% of the weight on the coarse modules (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}).
\item \textbf{Envelope.} Propagating the seed through coarse 16-pixel SA covers the whole object, even in cluttered scenes, but blurs its boundary; fine 64-pixel SA is precise but misses parts (Sec.~\ref{sec:envelope}).
\item \textbf{Detail within the envelope.} Passing from coarse to fine, we let each finer level move evidence only \emph{within} the mass its parent assigns, and never add mass beyond it (a one-sided cap). Enforcing exact conservation instead makes children inherit wrong parents' mass and fails (Sec.~\ref{sec:cap}).
\item \textbf{Boundary.} A fixed DenseCRF aligns the mask with image edges, and gains most once the cap has removed far-away mass (Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}).
\end{itemize}

The resulting pipeline uses no training, no optimized tokens and no tuned graph parameters. On VOC-Sim it reaches 0.829 out-of-fold mIoU, and on COCO-Cap it reaches 0.696 in frozen transfer, against 0.704 and 0.582 for OVAM with class words~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}. Every choice is made on VOC-Sim folds, with COCO-Cap used only after freezing.

Our contributions are: (i) a coarse-to-fine SA propagation with a one-sided mass cap, motivated and isolated by controlled ablations against mass-conserving and uncapped alternatives; (ii) a stage-by-stage account, with quantitative and qualitative evidence, of what the CA seed, coarse SA, fine SA and DenseCRF each contribute; (iii) an oracle threshold analysis showing that the hierarchy improves cross-image calibration rather than within-image ranking, which explains its main failure case; and (iv) a fold-safe selection protocol, together with the large negative record it produced.
