\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Why the search took this route.}
Each decision opened the next question. A selective seed (Sec.~\ref{sec:ca}) needed completion; completion by more propagation leaked, which motivated selective graph operators (Sec.~\ref{sec:sa}); the operators that helped were substitutes for the second pass and were absorbed by DenseCRF, which moved selection to the refined mask (Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion}); and selection on the refined mask found the middle-call window. Throughout, components that pass an independent screen can vanish in combination: head and call subsets disappear once crossed with the module choice, and the middle-call gain appears only after DenseCRF. Most of the record in Fig.~\ref{fig:decision_map} is negative, and the negatives kept the final pipeline simple.

\paragraph{Where the pipeline fails.}
The fixed-rule columns of Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline_stages} show the two characteristic failures. When the queried object shares SA affinity with co-occurring objects, propagation fills them too: the dining-table mask covers the chairs. When the seed is weak, propagation amplifies whatever it does mark: the COCO person seed spreads into the Christmas tree behind the figure. The class pattern across fourteen within-run comparisons agrees. On VOC-Sim, person has the largest mean loss and is the most volatile class (seven gains, seven losses); potted plant and bicycle, with thin and porous structures, lose far more often than they gain (8 and 6 losses against 1 and 3 gains). On COCO-Cap, dining table loses in nine of fourteen.

\paragraph{What the figures do and do not show.}
All qualitative examples are chosen by a stated rule before inspection. The deliberately opposing examples in Fig.~\ref{fig:temporal_tradeoff} show why a temporal restriction needs a cohort-level test. The first-arrival rates in Fig.~\ref{fig:propagation_depth} explain why deeper diffusion eventually leaks, but they come from a later package and do not prove a universal per-pixel confidence rule.

\paragraph{Selection and implementation boundaries.}
The main all-call pipeline and the later middle-call refinement were selected by their registered VOC rules. A3 received a favorable forced pairwise comparison after the multi-candidate selector kept A0; consequently it is exploratory, its full-VOC score is in-sample, and its gain holds only in the final pipeline (its CA-only mIoU falls). An independent code audit found that its resized SA affinity was transposed relative to the GoCA-inspired equation. On four images the correction changes per-call module weights by 0.9--2.0\% (relative), so a large change is unlikely, but until a corrected replay is evaluated the A3 number describes only the implemented operator. SG25 and SG27 further reuse that seed and inherit this limitation.

The benchmarks contain images generated from prompts and seeds, not natural-image segmentation benchmarks. Our regenerated images may differ from the renders annotated by OVAM, and some published baselines used their own generation settings. The literature table is therefore contextual, not a paired comparison. All choices are made on VOC-Sim and one diffusion backbone; the 20-class macro mean also hides class and image-level variation. A separate optimized-token track uses annotations and is not part of the training-free claim.

\section{Conclusion}
The most reliable training-free recipe in this study is a selective CA seed, observation-wise propagation through one fine SA module, two passes for object completion, and fixed image-aware refinement, with SA restricted to the middle calls once selection is made on the refined mask. Its failure mode is the tradeoff that shaped it: more propagation or coarser scale completes objects but also leaks.
