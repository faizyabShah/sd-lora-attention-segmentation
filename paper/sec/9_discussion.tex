\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\owner{Claude (draft from frozen results; Codex review requested, protocol Section 63)}

\paragraph{What the controlled path shows.}
Restrictions chosen on their own screen rarely survive: removing a
cross-attention head, keeping only the last denoising call, a subset of
self-attention resolutions or heads, and per-image Otsu thresholding each
passed or nearly passed alone and failed in combination. The changes that did
survive are simple: drop one cross-attention resolution, propagate through
one self-attention module, and refine with DenseCRF. The largest single lever
is the seed. OVAM's token raises the one-pass pipeline by 0.063 out-of-fold,
more than all propagation and refinement choices together on the class word
(0.026). DenseCRF is the most robust component: it is selected on VOC-Sim and
transfers to COCO-Cap with both the class word and the token.

\paragraph{Why a second propagation pass helps.}
\owner{Claude (AN1)}
Decomposing the error by pass shows that the second pass is pure filling: on
VOC-Sim it raises recall from 0.912 to 0.924 at unchanged precision, while
further passes lower precision. Its gain is concentrated in images whose seed
covers least of the object (the lowest third gains $+0.029$ image IoU, the
others under $+0.006$) and grows with object size; \emph{person} gains most.
The optimized token's seed already covers 94\% of the object, against 84\% for
the class word, which is why the token pipeline does not select a second pass.
Tracking the pixels each pass adds confirms that the second pass genuinely
fills: a quarter of its new object pixels lie more than 16 pixels from the
previous mask, and the false positives it adds are offset by those it removes.
On COCO-Cap, by contrast, it adds 2.3 false-positive pixels per new object
pixel (0.6 on VOC-Sim), farther from the object and twice as often in separate
regions, so the second pass does not transfer.
\source{protocol Sections 57--58; registry AN1}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\owner{Claude (draft)}
Both benchmarks are generated images: VOC-Sim and COCO-Cap are rendered by
Stable Diffusion~1.5 from released prompts and seeds. Our scores are on
regenerated images and released annotations, while the released baseline masks
were produced on their authors' renders and, for Attn2Mask, Grounded Diffusion
and DatasetDM, on their own image sets. All choices are made on one dataset
(VOC-Sim, 490 images, about 25 per class), so per-class conclusions are noisy.
COCO-Cap is scored with OVAM's VOC tokens because no COCO-Cap tokens were
released. Finally, all experiments use one model; whether the selected modules
transfer to other diffusion models or to domain-adapted ones is open.
\source{protocol Sections 41, 48, 54--56; registry R1, R10}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\owner{Claude (draft)}
Read out under a controlled, cross-validated selection, the attention of a
text-to-image diffusion model segments objects well with few, simple choices.
One self-attention module propagates a cross-attention seed, DenseCRF refines
it, and a better seed matters more than a deeper propagation. The resulting
training-free pipeline transfers to COCO-Cap without tuning, and an optimized
token closes much of the remaining gap.
