\begin{abstract}
Text-to-image diffusion models localize words in cross-attention (CA) and group pixels in self-attention (SA), so the images they generate can be segmented without training. We show that \emph{how these signals are combined across spatial scales} matters more than which graph operator is applied to them. Our training-free pipeline builds a class-word CA seed from the coarse-dominated modules, weighted by their agreement with SA. It then propagates the seed coarse to fine through SA at 16, 32 and 64 pixels. A one-sided cap lets each finer level redistribute evidence only within the mass its coarse parent assigns, and a fixed DenseCRF aligns the result with image edges. Each stage has a measurable role. Coarse SA supplies a high-recall object envelope that is robust to clutter, and fine SA restores detail. The cap removes 13\% of background mass but only 0.5\% of object mass, whereas exact mass conservation forces a wrong parent's mass onto its children and costs up to five points. DenseCRF gains six times more on capped maps in cluttered scenes. Selecting every choice on five class-stratified folds of VOC-Sim, the pipeline reaches 0.829 out-of-fold mIoU and transfers frozen to COCO-Cap at 0.696, against 0.704 and 0.582 for class-word OVAM. An oracle analysis shows that the hierarchy's gain is cross-image calibration of the propagated maps, which also explains its one systematic failure, objects that fill the frame.
\end{abstract}
