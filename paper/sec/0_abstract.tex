\begin{abstract}
Text-to-image diffusion models contain semantic localization in cross-attention and spatial grouping in self-attention. Turning these signals into masks raises a practical question: which observations and refinements help beyond a strong training-free reference, and which merely fit evaluation images? We study class-word segmentation on 490 VOC-Sim images with five class-stratified folds and transfer each frozen choice to 707 COCO-Cap images. A controlled progression retains cross-attention at 8, 16 and 64 pixels, propagates its seed through one 64-pixel self-attention module, applies two observation-wise passes, and refines the mask with a fixed DenseCRF. It reaches 0.813 out-of-fold mean IoU on VOC-Sim and 0.637 on COCO-Cap. Most of 45 registered comparisons fail: selective graph operators do not help, and observation-reliability operators that help one-pass propagation substitute for the second pass and are absorbed by DenseCRF. Selecting on the refined mask instead finds one surviving change, SA calls 10--20, reaching 0.816 out-of-fold and 0.644 on COCO-Cap. We analyze why a second pass completes objects while later arrivals are increasingly false positives. A separate exploratory attention-weighted seed reaches 0.823 in a forced out-of-fold comparison and 0.662 on COCO-Cap; we distinguish this from the registered selection path. Published OVAM and other baselines are reported under their original evaluation conditions. The results show that extraction, propagation and refinement must be selected jointly, with a clear boundary between validated gains and promising follow-up observations.
\end{abstract}
