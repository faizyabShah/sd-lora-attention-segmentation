\section{Boundary refinement and joint temporal selection}
\label{sec:fusion}
\emph{Question: what does refinement add, and does a gain on the propagated map survive it?} Propagation changes object extent; the final mask still needs a threshold and image-aware boundaries. We compared seed mixing, multiplicative gating, several normalizations and per-image Otsu calibration against the frozen propagation pair. Some calibration variants raise overall mIoU but violate the registered class-loss guard. The fixed DenseCRF is chosen in all five folds, improves all twenty classes in its screen, and raises OOF mIoU from 0.788 to 0.805 in that experiment. When two-pass propagation and DenseCRF are confirmed together, the combined system reaches 0.813 OOF and is selected in all five folds. The difference between the single-axis and combined gains is why we confirm interactions rather than adding isolated deltas.

\paragraph{Refinement absorbs propagation gains.}
We then carried the promoted propagation operators into the refined pipeline, each against two passes plus DenseCRF in the same run. None survived (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision_map}b). SG2 plus DenseCRF scores 0.811 forced OOF against 0.813 and wins no fold; SG12 plus DenseCRF scores 0.812 and is retained by the reference in all five folds. The SG18 middle-call window, which had won all five raw folds, also loses after DenseCRF with one pass (0.811 versus 0.814, no fold), although it gains 0.9 COCO-Cap points. Gains measured on the propagated map are therefore not additive with refinement. The decision changed our procedure: from here on, every candidate is selected on the \emph{refined} mask. It also left one cell untested, the middle-call window \emph{with} two passes and DenseCRF.

\paragraph{Time and refinement interact.}
The factorial study SG22 fills that cell: it crosses SA calls 0--30 versus 10--20 with one, two and three propagation passes, before and after R2. On raw maps, the registered selector keeps all calls. After R2, it chooses calls 10--20 with two passes in four of five folds, and the selection path scores 0.8158 OOF against 0.8132 for the same-generation ALL-P2+R2 reference. Evaluated in every held-out fold, MID-P2+R2 scores 0.8168 forced OOF, and 0.6438 versus 0.6372 on frozen COCO-Cap. The result supports a specific sequence---select temporal evidence for the \emph{refined} mask---rather than a general assertion that calls 21--30 are poor. In a separate diagnostic, late calls both recover some true pixels and add distant false positives; their utility varies by class and scene.
