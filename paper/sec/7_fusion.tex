\section{Boundary refinement and joint temporal selection}
\label{sec:fusion}
Propagation changes object extent; the final mask still needs a threshold and image-aware boundaries. We compared seed mixing, multiplicative gating, several normalizations and per-image Otsu calibration against the frozen propagation pair. Some calibration variants raise overall mIoU but violate the registered class-loss guard. The fixed DenseCRF is chosen in all five folds, improves all twenty classes in its screen, and raises OOF mIoU from 0.788 to 0.805 in that experiment. When two-pass propagation and DenseCRF are confirmed together, the combined system reaches 0.813 OOF and is selected in all five folds. The difference between the single-axis and combined gains is why we confirm interactions rather than adding isolated deltas.

\paragraph{Time and refinement interact.}
A later factorial study crosses SA calls 0--30 versus 10--20 with one, two and three propagation passes, before and after R2. On raw maps, the registered selector keeps all calls. After R2, it chooses calls 10--20 with two passes in four of five folds. This MID-P2+R2 setting scores 0.8168 OOF versus 0.8132 for same-generation ALL-P2+R2, and 0.6438 versus 0.6372 on frozen COCO-Cap. The result supports a specific sequence---select temporal evidence for the \emph{refined} mask---rather than a general assertion that calls 21--30 are poor. In a separate diagnostic, late calls both recover some true pixels and add distant false positives; their utility varies by class and scene.
