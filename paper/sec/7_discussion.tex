\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Negative results.}
We also registered 45 comparisons of alternative operators, such as random walks, graph sharpening and re-seeding between scales (supplementary material). Several improved the raw map, but apart from the SA call window none survived DenseCRF. The capped hierarchy is the largest change that survived refinement in every fold and transferred.

\paragraph{Failure modes and scope.}
The pipeline inherits SA's notion of an object. When co-occurring objects share coarse affinity (the dining table and its chairs, Figs.~\ref{fig:stages} and \ref{fig:cap}) or the seed is weak (COCO-Cap person, our weakest class at 0.28 IoU), propagation fills the wrong region, and frame-filling objects lose under one global threshold (Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration}). Both benchmarks contain generated images, regenerated from released prompts and seeds, and all choices are made on VOC-Sim with one backbone. Two components were adopted after the registered rule kept a simpler reference (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}); their gains hold in 4/5 and 5/5 held-out folds and transfer, but they are not pre-registered promotions.

\section{Conclusion}
Training-free segmentation from diffusion attention works best when each scale does what it is good at: coarse attention decides where the object is, finer SA refines its boundary within the mass the coarse level allows, and DenseCRF aligns it with the image. The one-sided cap is what makes this division of labour work.
