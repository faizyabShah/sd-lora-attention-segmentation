\section{Per-class effects}
\label{sec:supp_negative}
Figure~\ref{fig:supp_class_deltas} shows the per-class change of fourteen within-run comparisons, from the committed per-class tables (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}narmyn/\allowbreak{}attention/\allowbreak{}cross\_\allowbreak{}package\_\allowbreak{}classes.py}). Three observations support the main text. First, the promoted raw-stage operators (top block) resemble the second pass on VOC-Sim: over the 20 classes, the change from SG2 correlates with the change from a second pass at 0.88 and that from SG12 at 0.82 (SG11: 0.44; SG8: 0.34). This is consistent with their acting through the same mechanism, object completion. Second, after DenseCRF (bottom block) the pattern weakens, and only the middle-call window and A3 keep a positive VOC-Sim mean. Third, a change's per-class effect on VOC-Sim predicts its effect on COCO-Cap poorly: the correlation over classes is between $-0.01$ and $+0.46$ for every comparison. Person is the most volatile class on VOC-Sim, with seven gains and seven losses beyond $\pm0.005$; potted plant and chair lose most often (eight comparisons each), and bicycle loses twice as often as it gains; on COCO-Cap, dining table loses in nine of fourteen comparisons. Figure~\ref{fig:supp_temporal_classes} gives the class detail of the SG22 middle-call decision.
