\section{Semantic localization: selecting the CA seed}
\label{sec:ca}
A CA map should identify a reliable semantic core before any SA expansion. We first hold propagation aside and screen denoising calls, resolutions, modules, heads and attribution conventions. This separates a weak seed from an overly aggressive graph operation.

\paragraph{Spatial scale matters more than a preferred head.}
Removing the five $32\times32$ CA modules raises OOF mIoU from 0.665 to 0.691 in the combined CA confirmation; four folds choose it. The paired mean-image IoU change is $+0.022$ (95\% bootstrap interval $[+0.014,+0.029]$). The effect persists at 0.35, 0.45 and 0.55, although average precision falls from 0.922 to 0.913: the chosen seed is more selective at the operating threshold, not a universal improvement in pixel ranking. Figure~\ref{fig:ca_example} shows why scale is a substantive choice: the example's 32-pixel map highlights only a narrow part of the airplane, while the retained scales offer complementary extent and detail. This is an illustration, not evidence that every 32-pixel layer is poor.

On separate screens, removing CA head 1 and some module subsets appeared useful. Once crossed with the resolution choice, the extra head restriction changes mIoU by only $+0.0004$ and lies inside the 0.002 tie band. We therefore keep all eight heads. The conditional-query attribution convention, with the softmax over 77 text positions and the mean of all pieces of the class word, also survives; the tested OVAM-style unconditional/first-piece alternative is 0.016 lower in its own screen. Denoising-call restriction is not retained in this initial CA stage.
