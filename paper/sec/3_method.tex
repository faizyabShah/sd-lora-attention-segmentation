\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Given an image generated by a text-to-image diffusion model and a class word $w$ (\eg ``sheep''), we predict a binary mask of the pixels that belong to $w$. All signals are attention maps recorded while the image is generated, and no parameter is trained. The method has four steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). First, cross-attention gives a class-word map in every attention layer (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_ca}). Second, these maps are combined into a semantic seed, weighting each layer by how well its spatial structure agrees with self-attention (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_weight}). Third, the seed is propagated through self-attention from coarse to fine resolution, with each finer level capped by the coarser one (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_c2f}). Fourth, the map is thresholded and refined with DenseCRF (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_crf}).

\subsection{Preliminaries: attention in latent diffusion}
\label{sec:prelim}
Stable Diffusion~\cite{rombach2022ldm} generates an image in a latent space of size $64\times64$ (for a $512\times512$ image). Starting from Gaussian noise, a UNet denoises the latent over $T$ steps; we use $T{=}30$ and record its $31$ forward calls, indexed by $t$. The UNet contains transformer blocks at four spatial resolutions, $s\in\{64,32,16,8\}$. At call $t$, block $b$ receives image features $h_{b,t}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times C_b}$, one row per latent position, where $n_b=s_b^2$. The prompt is encoded by the text encoder $\tau_\theta$ into $X\in\mathbb R^{77\times d_E}$, one row per token. Each block contains a self-attention layer followed by a cross-attention layer, and each layer has $H{=}8$ heads. For head $h$,
\begin{equation}
A_h=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{Q_hK_h^\top}{\sqrt d}\Big),\quad Q_h=h_{b,t}W^{Q}_{h},\ \ K_h=Y W^{K}_{h},
\label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
where $W^Q_h, W^K_h$ are the layer's learned projections, $d$ is the head dimension and the softmax runs over each row. The keys come from $Y=h_{b,t}$ in self-attention and from text in cross-attention. The two attention matrices therefore mean different things:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \textbf{Cross-attention (CA)}, $A^{\text{ca}}_{b,t,h}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times77}$. Row $i$ is a distribution over text tokens for latent position $i$, so column $k$ is a spatial map of where token $k$ is attended~\cite{tang2023daam}.
\item \textbf{Self-attention (SA)}, $A^{\text{sa}}_{b,t,h}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times n_b}$. Row $i$ says from which positions position $i$ gathers features. Rows sum to one, so for any map $x\in\mathbb R^{n_b}$ the product $A^{\text{sa}}x$ replaces each value by an affinity-weighted average of the values at related positions. Repeated products therefore spread $x$ over regions that the network groups together.
\end{itemize}
Following OVAM~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}, the CA keys are computed from a separate \emph{attribution prompt} $X'$ (``a photograph of a [class]''), which contains $w$ even when the generation prompt does not. We write $\bar A^{s}_{t}=\frac1H\sum_hA^{\text{sa}}_{b,t,h}$ for the head-averaged SA matrix of the last decoder block at resolution $s$, and use $U$ for bilinear $2\times$ upsampling and $D$ for area downsampling.

\subsection{Class-word cross-attention maps}
\label{sec:method_ca}
For each CA layer $b$ and call $t$, the class-word map averages the heads and the columns of all tokenizer pieces $k\in\mathcal K_w$ of $w$:
\begin{equation}
a_{b,t}=\frac1H\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac1{|\mathcal K_w|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_w}A^{\text{ca}}_{b,t,h}[:,k]\ \in\mathbb R^{n_b}.
\label{eq:ca}
\end{equation}
We use the queries of the conditional (text-guided) branch. Stable Diffusion~1.5 has sixteen CA layers: five each at 64, 32 and 16 pixels (two in the encoder, three in the decoder) and one at 8 pixels. We use the eleven layers at 8, 16 and 64 pixels, $\mathcal B$, and discard the 32-pixel layers, which hurt in every combination (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}).

\subsection{Self-attention-guided layer weighting}
\label{sec:method_weight}
Not every CA layer is equally useful. Fine 64-pixel layers respond to texture and edges, coarse layers to whole objects (see the supplementary material). Averaging them uniformly gives the noisy fine layers five of eleven votes. We instead weight each layer by how well the spatial grouping of its block agrees with a reference grouping of the image, adapting the agreement score of GoCA~\cite{meng2026making} to every denoising call.

\paragraph{Reference grouping.} At call $t$ we take the CA queries $F_t\in\mathbb R^{n_r\times d_F}$ of one decoder block at 32 pixels (heads concatenated, $n_r{=}1024$). Their pairwise similarity
\begin{equation}
P_t=\operatorname{softmax}\!\big(F_tF_t^\top/\sqrt{d_F}\big)\ \in\mathbb R^{n_r\times n_r}
\label{eq:reference}
\end{equation}
is a pseudo-affinity: entry $(i,j)$ is large when positions $i$ and $j$ carry similar decoder features.

\paragraph{Agreement score.} For each CA layer $b\in\mathcal B$, let $S_{b,t}=\frac1H\sum_hA^{\text{sa}}_{b,t,h}$ be the head-averaged SA matrix of the \emph{same} transformer block. We resize it to $n_r\times n_r$ as GoCA does (bilinear along keys, nearest along queries) and write the result, with rows indexed by keys, as $\tilde S_{b,t}$.\footnote{GoCA indexes rows by queries. On our data the two orientations give seeds with Pearson correlation 0.99999 and the same results (supplementary material).} The layer's weight is its normalized, non-negative agreement with the reference:
\begin{equation}
w_{b,t}=\frac{\big[\langle\tilde S_{b,t},P_t\rangle\big]_+}{\sum_{b'\in\mathcal B}\big[\langle\tilde S_{b',t},P_t\rangle\big]_+},
\qquad \langle S,P\rangle=\sum_{i,j}S_{ij}P_{ij},
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_+=\max(\cdot,0)$. A block whose self-attention groups the image like the reference receives a large weight, and the weights at each call sum to one.

\paragraph{Semantic seed.} The seed sums the weighted class-word maps over layers and calls at $64\times64$:
\begin{equation}
c=\operatorname{norm}\Big(\frac1{31}\sum_{t=0}^{30}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}w_{b,t}\,U_{64}(a_{b,t})\Big)\ \in[0,1]^{64\times64},
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
where $U_{64}$ resizes bilinearly to $64\times64$ and $\operatorname{norm}$ rescales to $[0,1]$ (min--max). On average, the weights place 84\% of the mass on the 8- and 16-pixel layers (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}).

\subsection{Coarse-to-fine self-attention propagation}
\label{sec:method_c2f}
The seed $c$ marks the most class-specific parts of the object and leaves the rest weak (Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}). Propagating it with SA completes the object. Instead of propagating with fine 64-pixel SA alone, we pass the map through SA at 16, 32 and 64 pixels in turn, using the middle SA calls $t\in\mathcal T=\{10,\dots,20\}$, which were selected for the fine-only baseline (supplementary material). Maps are flattened when multiplied by an SA matrix. For each call:
\begin{align}
r_t&=\bar A^{16}_t\,D(c)&&\in\mathbb R^{16\times16}&&\text{(coarse envelope)}\label{eq:coarse}\\
q_t&=\bar A^{32}_t\,U(r_t)&&\in\mathbb R^{32\times32}&&\text{(finer proposal)}\label{eq:proposal}\\
z_t&=\operatorname{cap}(q_t;\,r_t)&&\in\mathbb R^{32\times32}&&\text{(capped proposal)}\label{eq:capped}
\end{align}
and the final map averages the fine propagation of the capped maps over calls:
\begin{equation}
y=\frac1{|\mathcal T|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\bar A^{64}_t\,U(z_t)\ \in\mathbb R^{64\times64}.
\label{eq:final}
\end{equation}
$D(c)$ averages each $4\times4$ block of the seed. Coarse SA in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coarse} spreads it over whole regions that the network groups together. Eq.~\eqref{eq:proposal} redistributes this evidence at 32 pixels, where boundaries are sharper but affinities can also reach look-alike background.

\paragraph{One-sided cap.} Every coarse cell $g$ has four children $\mathcal C(g)$ at the next resolution. Because bilinear upsampling preserves local means, the parent's value $r_g$ is the mean evidence that the coarse level assigns to those children. The cap lowers any group whose mean exceeds that budget and leaves all other groups unchanged:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{cap}(q;r)_k=q_k\cdot\min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{r_{g(k)}}{\bar q_{g(k)}}\Big),\qquad \bar q_g=\frac14\sum_{k\in\mathcal C(g)}q_k,
\label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
where $g(k)$ is the parent of child $k$ (a small $\epsilon{=}10^{-12}$ is added to $\bar q_g$). Within a capped group all children are scaled by the same factor, so their relative values are kept. The removed mass is discarded, not moved elsewhere. Equation~\eqref{eq:cap} is the closest map to $q$, in generalized KL divergence, that satisfies the upper bounds $\sum_{k\in\mathcal C(g)}z_k\le4r_g$ (proof in the supplementary material). It treats the coarse level as an \emph{upper} bound on where the object can be. The natural alternative, \emph{exact conservation} ($z_k=q_k\,r_{g(k)}/\bar q_{g(k)}$ for every group), also forces children to fill a parent's budget, even when the coarse map wrongly made that parent confident. Sec.~\ref{sec:cap} shows that only the one-sided version helps. We cap the $16\to32$ step only; the 64-pixel step stays free so that fine SA can sharpen boundaries.

\subsection{Mask prediction and boundary refinement}
\label{sec:method_crf}
The map $y$ is rescaled to $[0,1]$, upsampled bilinearly to image size and thresholded at a single global threshold $\tau$, which is chosen on the training folds (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}; $\tau{=}0.55$). This gives a binary mask $b$. Because SA operates on a $64\times64$ grid, the boundary of $b$ is blocky. DenseCRF~\cite{krahenbuhl2011crf} aligns it with the image by minimizing, over pixel labels $x_i\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{align}
E(x)&=\textstyle\sum_i\psi_u(x_i)+\sum_{i<j}[x_i\neq x_j]\,k(i,j),\label{eq:crf}\\
k(i,j)&=w_1\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{\|p_i-p_j\|^2}{2\theta_\alpha^2}-\tfrac{\|I_i-I_j\|^2}{2\theta_\beta^2}\Big)\nonumber\\
&\quad+w_2\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{\|p_i-p_j\|^2}{2\theta_\gamma^2}\Big).\nonumber
\end{align}
The unary term $\psi_u$ comes from $b$. The pairwise term penalizes giving different labels to pixels $i,j$ that are close in position $p$ and similar in colour $I$ (first kernel), or simply close (second kernel), so the mask boundary moves onto image edges. We use OVAM's fixed parameters ($w_1{=}10$, $\theta_\alpha{=}80$, $\theta_\beta{=}13$, $w_2{=}3$, $\theta_\gamma{=}3$, five iterations)~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}. No ground truth enters any step.
