\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Given an image generated by a text-to-image diffusion model and a class word $w$ (\eg ``sheep''), we predict a mask of the pixels that belong to $w$. Everything we use is attention recorded while the image is generated, and nothing is trained. The method has four steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Cross-attention gives a map of $w$ in every layer (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_ca}). These maps are combined into a seed, weighting each layer by how well it agrees with self-attention (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_weight}). The seed is propagated through self-attention from coarse to fine resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_c2f}). Finally, the map is thresholded and refined with DenseCRF (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_crf}).

\subsection{Preliminaries: attention in latent diffusion}
\label{sec:prelim}
Stable Diffusion~\cite{rombach2022ldm} generates a $512\times512$ image in a $64\times64$ latent space. A UNet removes noise from the latent over 30 denoising steps, guided by the prompt embedding from a text encoder. The UNet contains transformer blocks at four resolutions (64, 32, 16 and 8 pixels). Each block has a self-attention layer followed by a cross-attention layer. Both compute
\begin{equation}
A=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt d}\Big),
\label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
where the queries $Q$ come from the image features (one row per latent position), $d$ is the feature dimension, and each row of $A$ sums to one. The keys $K$ are what distinguishes the two layers:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item In \textbf{cross-attention (CA)}, $K$ comes from the text tokens. Row $i$ of $A$ says how strongly image position $i$ attends to each token, so the column of a token is a spatial map of that token~\cite{tang2023daam}.
\item In \textbf{self-attention (SA)}, $K$ also comes from the image, so $A$ is an affinity between positions. For a map $x$, the product $Ax$ replaces each value by a weighted average over the positions it attends to. Repeating it spreads $x$ over regions that the network groups together.
\end{itemize}
Each layer has eight heads; we always average them. Following OVAM~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}, the CA keys come from a separate attribution prompt (``a photograph of a [class]''), so $w$ can be queried even when it is absent from the generation prompt.

\subsection{Class-word cross-attention maps}
\label{sec:method_ca}
For a CA layer $\ell$, the map of $w$ is the column of $w$ in that layer's attention, averaged over heads and over the tokenizer pieces of $w$:
\begin{equation}
a_\ell=\operatorname{mean}_{\text{heads, pieces of }w}\ A_\ell[:,w].
\label{eq:ca}
\end{equation}
Stable Diffusion~1.5 has sixteen CA layers: five each at 64, 32 and 16 pixels and one at 8 pixels. We use the eleven layers at 8, 16 and 64 pixels and discard the 32-pixel layers, which hurt in every combination (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}).

\subsection{Self-attention-guided layer weighting}
\label{sec:method_weight}
The CA layers are not equally useful. The fine 64-pixel layers respond to texture and edges, while the coarse ones respond to whole objects. A uniform average still gives the noisy fine layers five of the eleven votes. We instead weight each layer by how well the self-attention of its block agrees with a reference grouping of the image, adapting the agreement score of GoCA~\cite{meng2026making}.

The reference $P=\operatorname{softmax}(FF^\top/\sqrt d)$ compares the query features $F$ of one decoder block at 32 pixels with each other. $P_{ij}$ is large when positions $i$ and $j$ look alike to the decoder. For CA layer $\ell$, let $S_\ell$ be the self-attention of the same block, resized to the size of $P$.\footnote{As in GoCA, we resize keys bilinearly and queries by nearest neighbour. GoCA's orientation of $S_\ell$ is the transpose of ours; on our data the two give seeds with Pearson correlation 0.99999 and the same results (supplementary material).} The weight of the layer is its agreement with $P$, clipped at zero and normalized over the layers:
\begin{equation}
w_\ell=\frac{\max(0,\langle S_\ell,P\rangle)}{\sum_{m}\max(0,\langle S_m,P\rangle)},\qquad
c=\operatorname{norm}\Big(\sum_\ell w_\ell\,a_\ell\Big).
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
Here $\langle S,P\rangle=\sum_{ij}S_{ij}P_{ij}$, every map $a_\ell$ is first resized to $64\times64$, and $\operatorname{norm}$ rescales to $[0,1]$. The weights and the weighted sum are computed at every denoising step and averaged over the 31 steps to give the seed $c$. A layer receives a large weight when its self-attention groups the image like the reference; in practice 84\% of the weight goes to the 8- and 16-pixel layers (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}).

\subsection{Coarse-to-fine self-attention propagation}
\label{sec:method_c2f}
The seed marks the most class-specific parts of the object and leaves the rest weak (Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}). Self-attention can complete it. Rather than propagating with fine 64-pixel SA alone, we pass the map through SA at 16, 32 and 64 pixels in turn. Let $S_{16}$, $S_{32}$ and $S_{64}$ denote the head-averaged SA of the last decoder block at each resolution, and $\uparrow$ bilinear $2\times$ upsampling. At each denoising step,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r&=S_{16}\,c_{16}, & q&=S_{32}\,{\uparrow}r,\\
z&=\operatorname{cap}(q,r), & y&=S_{64}\,{\uparrow}z,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:c2f}
\end{equation}
where $c_{16}$ is the seed averaged down to $16\times16$. The final map averages $y$ over the middle steps 10--20, the window selected for fine-only propagation (supplementary material). Coarse SA turns the seed into an \emph{envelope} $r$ that covers whole regions the network groups together. The 32-pixel SA turns $r$ into a sharper \emph{proposal} $q$, whose affinities can also reach look-alike background. The cap removes that excess, and 64-pixel SA restores fine detail.

\paragraph{One-sided cap.} Each coarse cell $g$ of $r$ has four children in $q$. Because upsampling preserves local means, $r_g$ is the average evidence that the coarse level allows for those children. If the children's mean $\bar q_g$ exceeds it, all four are scaled down by the same factor; otherwise they are left unchanged:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{cap}(q,r)_k=q_k\cdot\min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{r_g}{\bar q_g}\Big)\qquad\text{for each child }k\text{ of }g.
\label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
The removed mass is discarded, not moved elsewhere, and relative values within a group are kept. Equation~\eqref{eq:cap} is the closest map to $q$, in generalized KL divergence, whose groups do not exceed their parent's budget (proof in the supplementary material). The coarse level thus acts as an \emph{upper} bound on where the object can be. The natural alternative, \emph{exact conservation}, scales every group to exactly $r_g$. It therefore also forces children to fill the budget of a parent that the blurry coarse map wrongly made confident. Section~\ref{sec:cap} shows that only the one-sided version helps. We cap only the step from 16 to 32 pixels; the 64-pixel step is left free so that fine SA can sharpen boundaries.

\subsection{Mask prediction and boundary refinement}
\label{sec:method_crf}
The final map is rescaled to $[0,1]$, upsampled to image size and thresholded at one global value $\tau$, chosen on the training folds (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}; $\tau{=}0.55$). Because SA works on a $64\times64$ grid, the mask boundary is blocky. DenseCRF~\cite{krahenbuhl2011crf} aligns it with the image by choosing pixel labels $x$ that minimize
\begin{equation}
E(x)=\sum_i\psi_u(x_i)+\sum_{i<j}\psi_p(x_i,x_j).
\label{eq:crf}
\end{equation}
The unary term $\psi_u$ keeps each pixel close to the thresholded mask. The pairwise term $\psi_p$ penalizes different labels for two pixels that are close and similar in colour (an appearance kernel) or simply close (a smoothness kernel), which moves the boundary onto image edges. We use OVAM's fixed parameters (appearance weight 10 with bandwidths 80 for position and 13 for colour, smoothness weight 3 with bandwidth 3, five iterations)~\cite{marcosmanchon2024ovam}. No ground truth enters any step.
