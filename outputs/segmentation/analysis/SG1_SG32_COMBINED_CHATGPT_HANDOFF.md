# Combined handoff: training-free attention segmentation, SG1–SG32

**One-time synthesis prepared 29 October 2026.** This document is intended to
be given to ChatGPT as a single, self-contained account of the experiments.
It distinguishes results from hypotheses. SG33 is in progress and has no
results here. The full source reports, machine-readable results and per-image
counts remain in this repository; representative paths are at the end.

## What we are trying to do

We segment a **queried class in an image generated by Stable Diffusion 1.5**
without training a segmentation model. A class-word cross-attention (CA) map
provides the semantic seed. Self-attention (SA) from the same generation
propagates that seed spatially. An unchanged DenseCRF (`R2`) can refine the
result. We have searched CA aggregation, SA time/layer/head/scale selection,
propagation depth, graph filtering, region labelling, fusion and postprocessing.
We currently focus on **training-free ordinary class words**. Experiments
using mask-optimized tokens are a separate, supervised support-image track.

The two benchmarks are **VOC-Sim: 490 images, 20 classes** and **COCO-Cap:
707 images, 20 classes**. VOC is the tuning/selection dataset. Five fixed
VOC folds choose methods and thresholds; a full-VOC threshold is then frozen
for COCO. COCO is transfer-only. In 465 COCO images the queried class word
appears in the **generation caption**; in 242 it does not. The separate
attribution prompt always contains the class word. VOC generation captions
contain the class. The primary score is 20-class **macro pooled mIoU**;
reported 20,000-resample paired intervals usually concern *mean per-image
IoU*, a different estimand.

**Readout discipline.** A candidate's forced five-fold out-of-fold (OOF)
score, its pairwise fold wins, and the registered multi-candidate selector
are three different results. A candidate can have a positive forced OOF
gain and still fail the magnitude/stability promotion guard. Comparing
methods evaluated on independently regenerated images or hardware is
descriptive; same-study controls are the reliable comparison. COCO cannot be
used to choose a cell or threshold. Multiple SG studies have an A3 CA
**attention-orientation caveat**: its pseudo-global layer weighting was
implemented consistently, but its CA layer similarity is transposed relative
to the source GoCA formula. Do not describe A3 as a GoCA reproduction.

## The current methodological spine

1. **Semantic source:** ordinary class-word CA. Historical OVAM-like W/REF
   is a useful baseline; early same-study scores were about **.788 VOC /
   .617 COCO**. The CA seed alone was about **.713/.563** in the early
   screen. Exact later reference values vary slightly by regeneration.
2. **Spatial propagation:** a second fine-resolution SA pass improved the
   early VOC baseline; `W/P1+R2` was about **.813 VOC / .637 COCO** in the
   earlier FTF screen. SA module 15 at 64×64 was used for fine propagation.
3. **Temporal window:** SA calls **10–20** (`MID`) with two passes and R2
   beat the original all-call two-pass final system in SG22: **.8168 vs
   .8132 forced VOC OOF**, with 5/5 pairwise wins and 4/5 selector votes;
   frozen COCO **.6438 vs .6372** in that study.
4. **CA construction:** A3 pseudo-global CA weighting, applied to the
   selected eleven CA modules and calls 0–30, yielded **.8230 forced VOC
   OOF / .8243 full VOC / .6620 COCO** in SG24-A versus A0 **.8169 /
   .8185 / .6437**. It had 4/5 pairwise fold wins, but the registered
   *multi-candidate* selector did **not** promote it. The owners explicitly
   chose A3 as the **working experimental baseline** for subsequent studies.
   That post-results working choice must not be relabelled a preregistered
   promotion. Its CA-only score was lower than A0 on VOC despite its better
   final score, demonstrating a CA×SA×R2 interaction.
5. **Current A3 fine-P2 reference:** across SG25–SG32, same-study
   `A3 → FINE-P2 → R2` is consistently about **.823 forced VOC OOF /
   .824 full VOC / .662 frozen COCO**. This is the reference for the later
   mechanistic screens. Some unpromoted coarse-scale alternatives transfer
   markedly better to COCO; do not call the fine reference the best observed
   COCO cell or use COCO to revise the registered VOC selection.

`R2` is the unchanged OVAM-parameter DenseCRF used in the registered
comparisons. A suffix `+R2` means final mask after that CRF; `raw` means
before CRF. `P1/P2/P3` indicate propagation depth within their own study.
Cell labels such as `S1` are **study-local**; SG30 S1 and SG31 S1 mean
different operators.

## The most informative mechanism findings

### 1. Object completion and false-positive leakage are the central tension

One extra SA propagation pass often fills missing target pixels and improves
VOC. More passes become increasingly impure. The Pre-SG26 first-arrival
diagnostic quantifies this: TP/FP falls from **6.38 at depth 1 to .29 at
depth 5 on VOC**, and **2.43 to .13 on COCO**. This is aggregated over
changed pixels and not a per-class guarantee. SG17's hard veto proves that
detecting some bad additions is insufficient if many useful additions are
also deleted. SG22 finds two passes with middle calls and R2 more useful
than three or unrestricted calls. The problem is a *budgeted completion*
decision, not simply “propagate more” or “block more.”

### 2. Cross-scale support is valuable, but fixed agreement is dataset-sensitive

In SG25, 16→64+R2 improves COCO precision/transfer (.6975 vs .6621 fine)
while losing VOC OOF. SG27 explains a plausible reason: among 64×64 pixels
where **only FINE** is high, the GT-positive fraction is **.591 VOC but .222
COCO**; where **both FINE and C16** are high, it is .897 VOC/.757 COCO.
Thus a rule that requires coarse agreement removes useful VOC fine-only
pixels but more COCO leakage. These are pooled diagnostic fractions, not
an inference-time decision rule. Fixed fusion/product/minimum methods show
the tradeoff, but no registered late-fusion cell was promoted.

### 3. Time changes signal quality, yet late support is not a free veto

One-pass calls 10–20 beat all calls and calls 10–30 on both datasets in
SG18. The pre-SG19 control then showed that raw middle score already
separates middle-added TP/FP better than late score; the distinctive
late-minus-CA, late-minus-middle and late-consensus signals were weak.
Therefore do not revive SG19 as an untested “late support” mechanism
without new evidence. Also, T1+CRF transfers better to COCO but fails the
VOC final-pipeline selector, showing time selection interacts with pass
count and postprocessing.

### 4. Spatial partitions are ahead of their semantic labels

SG4/SG15 partitions admit high GT-majority oracle masks: SG15 reports
**.8859 mean-image IoU** at annotation-resolution region labels on VOC,
whereas ordinary automatic SG13 region scoring was much lower (best R1
**.7673 mean-image IoU** on VOC). These are **mean-image**, not macro mIoU,
and an oracle uses GT only diagnostically. SG13's best R1 still misses
760 oracle-positive VOC regions and selects 1,177 oracle-negative regions.
The bottleneck is deciding which regions belong to the queried class,
especially ambiguous ones; better partitioning or crude graph spreading
alone has not fixed it. Pre-SG16 occupancy AUC falls from .951 on all VOC
regions to .657 on ambiguous regions.

### 5. Sharper affinity is not automatically a better semantic mask

SG30 second-pass inflation dramatically concentrated transition mass and
improved COCO transfer but hurt VOC OOF. SG32 made this spatially adaptive:
its combined signal gave mean row exponent **1.245 on VOC GT foreground
versus 1.782 on distant background**; the intended modulation happened.
Yet stronger adaptive temperature cells lost VOC OOF. SG32 three-scale
`pair` agreement was **.937 on foreground and .964 on distant background**:
agreement itself largely fails to identify target versus non-target.
Selected-row background support was more discriminative (.880 foreground
vs .545 distant background on VOC) but coupling it to SA row temperature
did not produce a promotion. This suggests testing the *decision made from*
background evidence, rather than just strengthening the same operator.

### 6. DenseCRF supplies some boundary gains, but is not a universal cure

R2 often raises final mIoU and can change which temporal/pass setting is
preferred. SG28's boundary-aware Laplacian benefit almost disappears after
R2, suggesting overlap. Conversely, SG2 and SG12 gains under one-pass
screening did not survive as final-system improvements. Any proposed new
component must be evaluated both raw and after unchanged R2, with the
final-pipeline reference chosen before COCO is read.

## What could work together: hypotheses, not results

These are **new experiments to design**, not combinations already shown to
win. They are ordered by how directly the existing evidence motivates them.
Please scrutinize whether each has a genuinely independent mechanism and a
diagnostic that could falsify it before another large run.

1. **Soft propagation budget from first-arrival depth and semantic support.**
   The Pre-SG26 depth gradient is strong, while SG17 hard rejection fails.
   Instead of a binary veto or more SA passes, estimate how much *second-pass
   residual* to retain at each pixel or region using the first-pass seed,
   arrival depth and SG32 background evidence. Preserve the P1 map as a
   lower bound, use a bounded correction, and measure TP retained per FP
   removed on VOC folds. This is different from SG32's row-temperature
   change and SG26's edge multiplication. First test whether the combined
   diagnostic separates useful from harmful P2 additions beyond P1 score
   alone; otherwise do not build the method.
2. **Conditional output-level choice between fine and coarse→fine routes.**
   SG25 coarse→fine helps COCO, while SG27 fine-only pixels are much purer
   on VOC. All SG27 fusions had fixed rules. A *predeclared, image- or
   region-conditioned* mixture could use class-word CA evidence and
   cross-scale disagreement to preserve fine-only support when it is
   independently credible, while demanding coarse support when it is not.
   Test the credibility signal's VOC/COCO transfer first. Do not use
   dataset identity, GT, class-specific weights, or COCO tuning at inference.
3. **Target-conditioned region labelling with an abstention mechanism.**
   SG4/15 oracle headroom is real, but SG13's mean/maximum CA and consensus
   rules failed. A new labeller would need an independent way to avoid
   falsely accepting contextual regions while preserving ambiguous target
   parts. A diagnostic should focus only on the difficult oracle-positive
   and oracle-negative regions, rather than reporting easy-region AUC.
   Avoid generic object discovery plus caption-word assignment; the queried
   class should remain the target. This is lower confidence than ideas 1–2.
4. **Check interaction with R2 and CA construction explicitly.** SG24 A3
   helped the final system even while CA-only VOC worsened. SG22/23 and SG28
   show later components can erase earlier gains. Any new mechanism should
   use a small factorial against the relevant same-generation controls:
   raw versus R2; A3 fine-P2 versus a deliberately frozen coarse route;
   and no method chosen by COCO score. Avoid a large Cartesian sweep
   without a distinctive diagnostic prediction.

SG33 already tests a separate residual-hierarchy/Markov hypothesis. Wait
for it before proposing another multi-scale residual or reachability method.

## What has repeatedly failed, or needs caution

- Hard seed selection, hard region projection, hard bottleneck, and hard
  addition veto often trade away too many true target pixels (SG10, SG13,
  SG15, SG17, SG31). A proposed “cleaner mask” should report retained TP,
  not just removed FP.
- Graph structure alone does not supply target identity. Reciprocal edges,
  head entropy/locality, sharper within-object mass and scale agreement
  can look favorable while final target IoU declines (SG9, SG21, SG29,
  SG30, SG32).
- Strong COCO scores from 16→64 and SG27 product are descriptive because
  VOC selected another cell. The product's endpoint threshold and monotone
  equivalence to a geometric product create calibration caveats.
- Results from optimized-token `T` tracks use mask information and should
  never be mixed into a training-free W-track comparison or used as evidence
  that an ordinary class-word method learned nothing.
- Different SG reports use different first-stage references and sometimes
  independent regenerations. Never subtract two reported scores from
  separate studies and call the result an ablation.

## A useful request to ChatGPT

Given the evidence above, propose **one or two genuinely independent,
training-free mechanisms** that address a measured failure mode. For each,
give: (i) the testable causal prediction; (ii) a diagnostic-only go/no-go
test on the relevant same-image signals; (iii) a very small locked cell set;
(iv) same-generation controls and raw/R2 evaluation; (v) five-fold VOC
selection with frozen COCO transfer; and (vi) what result would falsify the
idea. Distinguish new mechanisms from fixed weighting, threshold
calibration or a reformulation of SG17/26/27/32. Do not use optimized
tokens, learned weights, GT at inference, or COCO-selected rules.
